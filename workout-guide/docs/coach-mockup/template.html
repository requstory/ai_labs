FitSplit Coach

Your 24-week checklist, from Mon 9 Nov 2026 to Sun 25 Apr 2027, as a Home Screen app for your iPhone. It shows today's session with FitSplit's animated exercises, times your runs and sets, logs your Monday weigh-ins and VO₂ max, and reminds you the night before.

Try it

  1. Today shows Tue 10 Nov (Strength A) as the example date. Tap Start session.
  2. Plan covers all 24 weeks and 168 days, generated from your checklist. Tap any day.
  3. Progress has the Monday weigh-in, plus charts of your numbers against target.
  4. More holds reminders, your starting numbers, backup and reference routines.
  5. New in v2: the screen-on badge and music bar in sessions, and Import from Health on Progress.

The sample weigh-ins on the Progress tab are made up to show what the charts look like. Nothing you tap here is saved.

Build plan

How the app will be built

The app goes into the same Astro site as FitSplit, at requstory.github.io/ai_labs/coach/, with its own app manifest. In Safari you tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and it opens full screen as a separate app called “Coach”. It reuses FitSplit's 302 exercise frames, its tinted animation loop and its session timer and music. It works offline after the first visit.

Screens and navigation

ScreenWhat it does
Today (tab)Today's row from your checklist, an animated preview, Start session, this week's progress, and tomorrow's session with a "lay out your kit" prompt. On Mondays a weigh-in card appears at the top.
Plan (tab)A strip of the 24 weeks, with recovery weeks marked. Each day shows its type colour and status: done, missed or upcoming. Tapping a day pushes a Day screen with a back button.
Day (pushed)Warm-up, main session and cool-down written out, with every exercise animated. You can start the session or mark the day done from here.
Session (full-screen sheet)Strength: warm-up, then each exercise with a set tracker, a 60–90 s rest timer and a kg field that shows last time's weight, then the stretch routine. Runs and intervals: a jog/walk or hard/easy timer with spoken cues, your heart-rate zone, and a reminder to record it as Outdoor Run. It ends with a short log (duration, average heart rate, how it felt). Swipe down or tap ✕ to close; a confirmation stops you losing a session by accident.
Progress (tab)The Monday weigh-in and VO₂ max form, weight and VO₂ max charts against your targets with a 3-week trend line, and how many sessions you've completed.
More (tab)Reminders, your starting numbers, the reference routines (warm-up, stretch, heart rate, choosing weights, kit, missed days), backup and restore, and the safety guidance.

Each day has its own link (for example /coach/day/2026-11-10/), so a reminder opens straight to that day. The phone's back gesture returns to the previous screen. The tab bar stays fixed, and sessions open as sheets above it.

Your plan as data

  • The checklist becomes a small typed module covering the 24 weeks, 3 phases and 2 day patterns. From week 9, Wednesday is the rest day and Strength B moves to Sunday. Tests check that all 168 generated days match the text of your checklist word for word.
  • Every exercise is linked to a FitSplit animation. Goblet squat → goblet-squat, push-up on a bench → incline-push-up, shoulder press → standing-dumbbell-press, Romanian deadlift → dumbbell-romanian-deadlift, the shoulder-tap plank → plank-shoulder-tap, and so on. The warm-up and stretch routines are animated too. Two are stand-ins: good-morning for the hip hinge, and FitSplit's treadmill drawings for jogging and walking.
  • Missed days are shown as missed and are never moved, so you carry on from the next day without doubling up, as your checklist says.

Your data and privacy

  • Everything you log stays on your iPhone, in the app's own storage, and the app asks iOS to keep that storage. Apps added to the Home Screen are not subject to Safari's 7-day clean-up.
  • The repository is public, so your weight targets will not be written into the code. On first launch you enter your start weight (113 kg), start VO₂ max (26) and start date. The weekly targets (−0.7 kg and +0.5 VO₂ max per week) are worked out on the phone.
  • Back up saves a file to the Files app, and Restore loads it back, so you can move to a new phone or recover if you ever delete the app.
  • A web app can't read Apple Health. You type in the VO₂ max and weight from the Health app, which takes about 10 seconds on a Monday. Run time and average heart rate are optional fields after each session.

Daily notifications

On iPhone, a web app can only show notifications that a server pushes to it. There's no server in this setup yet, so there are two ways to do reminders:

OptionHow it worksTrade-offs
A. Calendar alerts (recommended to start)The app creates a calendar file with all 168 sessions. Each event holds the full instructions, a link to that day in the app, and alerts at the times you choose, e.g. 20:00 the night before and 07:00 on the day. You add it to the iPhone Calendar with one tap.No server and no cost, and it works offline. The alerts come from Calendar, not from the app, and they don't know whether you've already done the session. If you change the reminder times, you add a new calendar file.
B. Push notificationsYou allow notifications in the app (iOS 16.4 or later, Home Screen apps only). A free scheduled Cloudflare Worker sends you tomorrow's session at your chosen time.True app notifications that open the right day, and they can skip a reminder once the day is marked done. They need a free Cloudflare account and a one-time setup of push keys. Your device's push address is stored on that worker, but none of your health data is.

Decided: A (calendar alerts). B can be added later without changing anything else.

iPhone details

  • Full screen with no browser bars. Layout respects the notch, Dynamic Island and home indicator. Tap targets are at least 44 pt, the system font is used, and the app is dark to match FitSplit.
  • The screen stays awake during sessions where iOS allows it. Spoken cues use the same audio as FitSplit (iPhone has no web vibration). Timers keep correct time when you lock the phone and come back.
  • The app works with VoiceOver: labelled controls, announced timer phases, and visible focus. Animations stop when Reduce Motion is on.
  • The app checks for updates in the background and shows an "Update ready" banner. This fixes the stale-cache issue FitSplit had before.

Keeping the screen on

  • When a session starts, Coach asks iOS to keep the screen awake, using the Screen Wake Lock feature. This works for Home Screen web apps on current iOS. A green Screen on badge shows it's active.
  • iOS drops the lock when you leave the app or lock the phone. Coach asks for it again as soon as you come back, and the timers keep correct time in between.
  • The screen goes back to normal when the session ends or you close it, so your battery isn't drained the rest of the day.
  • If an iOS version ever refuses the wake lock, Coach falls back to a silent looping video, which also keeps the screen on.

Music: Spotify and YouTube Music

  • Spotify: yes, with Premium. You sign in once. Coach then shows play, pause, next and previous in the session, and can start a chosen playlist when a session starts, e.g. one for strength and one for runs. The music still plays in the Spotify app, and Coach sends it commands. A free account doesn't allow this.
  • YouTube Music: no direct control. Google offers no way for other apps to control it. Coach can show an Open YouTube Music button, and you use the Lock Screen or Control Center music controls as usual.
  • Voice cues over your music. Coach will play its cues over your music, briefly lowering it, instead of stopping it. iOS decides how web-app sound mixes with other apps, so I'll test this on a real iPhone before relying on it.
  • FitSplit's built-in tracks stay as an option if you don't want to use another app.

Build steps

  1. Plan data module and tests (all 168 days, dates, targets).
  2. App shell: manifest, icons, offline support, tab bar, day links.
  3. Today, Plan and Day screens with the animations.
  4. Session players: strength sets and rest, and run/interval timers.
  5. Logging and Progress: device storage, weigh-in, charts, backup and restore.
  6. Reminders: calendar file first, then push if you choose it.
  7. Checks on an emulated iPhone 15 (Playwright) and accessibility checks (axe), then deploy to GitHub Pages. You then install it from Safari.
